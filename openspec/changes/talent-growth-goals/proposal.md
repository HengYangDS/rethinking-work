## Why

Chapter 29 read as a rubric: a table of tier conditions, the conditions for D, evidence counts, a five-step procedure,
published statistics and promotion mechanics, followed by an example that walked them. The owner judged it mechanical
and asked for the chapter to state principles and spirit, to guide people upward rather than set up rules, to help each
member grow with clear goals rather than measure people, and not to treat people as objects. He then asked that the
grades and the five tiers still be explained, as goals, not laid out as procedure.

## What Changes

- Chapter 29 is retitled 「目标与认可」 and rewritten in five sections: the goals of the four stages and eight grades;
  the 成色 of a person's work, seen in the three things that stay with a person (chapter 31); recognition; the
  assessment of a period, with the five tiers read against the member's own grade, the next step each points to,
  promotion in one rule, the principles, support where a member falls short, and the result told to the member alone;
  and how to guide a member upward. The opening member's period ends with his tier and his next step.
- The tiers' detailed conditions, evidence counts, the procedure, statistics, the work score and the evidence for the
  two crossings are left to the department's guidelines, which the chapter names as their source.
- Three passages from Chinese classics carry the chapter's turns, each quoted exactly with its source and a plain
  gloss: 《传习录》 on 精金, 《礼记·学记》 on guiding, 《道德经》 chapter 17.
- Chapter 31's sentence that pointed to the chapter's "two sides" points to its 成色 instead.
- The requirement in `book-argument` is renamed and restated for the new purpose; the prose skill's evaluation terms
  follow the chapter.
