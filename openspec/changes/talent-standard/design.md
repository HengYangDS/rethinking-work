## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- A reader without access to the department's guidelines can apply the standard from the chapter alone.
- The book holds at most 200 pages with the chapter in, and a test holds the ceiling.

**Non-Goals:**

- Editing the department's guidelines or the recruitment playbook; they are other repositories.
- Anchors per tier beyond one example column; the guidelines defer them until a period's records exist.
- Shortening prose for its own sake; a cut removes a second statement of something stated once elsewhere.

## Decisions

- The chapter sits after chapter 28 and before chapter 29 (带领): chapter 28 measures whether the work improved, the new
  chapter reads a person from the work, and the leading chapter uses that reading for assignment, delegation and
  fairness. Chapter 28 keeps its definition of 突出, which chapter 29 already cites; the new chapter applies it.
- The tier table states each tier relative to the grade's written requirements, as the guidelines' r2 does. Until the
  owner approves r2, the book's sentences contradict neither version, because r1's leading clauses are the same.
- The page ceiling is declared once, beside the layout's other bounds; the test reads it, so changing the ceiling is one
  edit.
- Cuts follow the saved readings' high-confidence candidates first; a candidate is taken only when the other statement
  stays and carries the meaning.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Renumbering leaves a stale printed chapter number] → A search for printed numbers 29 to 31 after the edit, and the
  contents test.
- [A cut loses a qualification] → Each cut is read against the readings' protection list, and the changed passages get
  a cold read.
- [The chapter's tier wording diverges from the guidelines] → Its source is named in the Change and in the chapter; a
  later revision there is followed here by a Change of its own.
