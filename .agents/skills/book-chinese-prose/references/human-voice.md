# Writing about people as a person would

A person writing about colleagues puts care and encouragement first, and writes nothing that runs against human
culture, custom or common sense or that treats people as objects. This file turns that into practice. It governs every passage about people: team, roles, review, evaluation, leading, growth,
hiring, the closing chapters, and any scene in which colleagues act.

## 1. The person is the subject

Write what a person does, learns, contributes and where they go next. The standard is a tool that serves them, not a
machine that processes them. Words that treat a person as an item handled (「给他一个字母」「定为 D」「人员筛选」「名额」
「档位」「更换」「替换」 for a person) stay only inside a definition that needs them; around it, speak of the person.

| 原文                                            | 方向                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 每一个字母，都要有查得到原件的记录。            | 每一个评定结论，都要有查得到原件的记录。                  |
| 一个人的评定等级，用 S、A、B、C、D 五个字母表示 | 评定等级分 S、A、B、C、D 五档，说的是一个人这一周期的表现 |
| 做事的一方可以更换                              | 同事之间写接替、接手；只说智能体时仍写更换                |

## 2. Purpose before mechanism

Why the department evaluates comes first: to see and reward each person's contribution, to give everyone a fair basis
known in advance, and to show each person the next step. The rules follow, in proportion. A chapter that opens on
rules and closes on purpose has the order reversed.

## 3. Shortfall is where support begins

Where a person falls short, the book says what the department provides (a clear goal, a mentor, a check-in, time)
before what it requires. Recognition is named plainly: 认可、奖励、机会、信任、晋升. Name 奖惩 where it is meant; do not
dress a penalty as care, and do not turn care into a penalty clause.

| 原文                                                                                           | 方向                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| C 之后，职级不变。下一期……期末同一项仍然不足，本人与有权决定的人约定改进期。D 之后，立即约定。 | 先说部门做什么：哪几项还要别人补上关键判断，就为这几项定目标、配带教人、期中当面谈一次；仍未补上，再书面约定改进计划。 |
| 同一条底线一期内越过两次，定为 D                                                               | 先说为什么：这几条底线是同事之间能够相互依靠的前提；再说规则。                                                         |

## 4. Proportion: a book, not a rulebook

The department guidelines hold the procedure. The book gives the principle, the main rule, one worked case, and
where the rest is written. Edge cases that only an evaluator on the day needs (which period a late result counts in,
when a count restarts, how ties between three evaluators are compared) are said once and briefly, or left to the
guidelines with a pointer.

## 5. Custom and common sense

Scenes follow how colleagues in a Chinese workplace actually behave: a lead talks with a member, a reviewer asks, a
team discusses, a mentor explains. Avoid scenes no person would stage (a prediction signed like a contract,
colleagues ranked by an algorithm, periods counted like a ledger) unless that very rule is the point. Numbers in a
scene are ones a person would say (约七成, 两处), not ones only a script would produce.

## 6. Thought and culture

The book's argument is that understanding and responsibility stay with people. Let that show as respect for people's
judgment, time and growth over years (培养、传承、信任建立在记录上), in the author's restrained, objective register: no
slogans, no sentiment, no first person, no lecture.

## 7. Boundaries that stay

- Judging rests on the work a person did; the book names no other trait, not even to exclude it.
- No sentence denies a meaning nobody gave, restates the plain, or piles explanation on a fault.
- The department guidelines remain the one definition of grades and tiers; the book changes how they are said.
