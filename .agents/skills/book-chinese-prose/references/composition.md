# Word choice and composition

The shared definition of 雅 governs this file: on top of fidelity and clarity, the wording, the structure, the rhythm
and transitions of the prose, and the layout suit the subject and the reader; restraint, concision, order and
consistency are its means, not its definition. When this file and fidelity or clarity disagree, fidelity and clarity
prevail. What follows is the practice the owner asked for on 2026-11-26 (「遣词造句，谋篇布局」).

## Words and sentences (遣词造句)

- **The exact word.** Choose the word whose dictionary sense is the thing meant, and the concrete verb over the general
  one: 查明、核对、交回、补写 rather than 做、进行、处理, where the act is known.
- **No near repetition.** A word that is not a term appears once in adjacent clauses; vary by recasting the sentence,
  never by a synonym for a term in `terms.md`.
- **Rhythm.** Mix short and long sentences. Split a sentence that carries more than two qualifications; join short
  sentences that repeat one subject.
- **Modifiers.** At most two 的 before a noun; a longer modifier becomes a clause after it.
- **Parallels.** Items set side by side share one grammatical shape and one weight; a list is as long as its content,
  not padded to three or cut to fit.
- **Connectives.** Name a relation (因为、所以、但、而、于是) only where the reader needs it; a paragraph whose sentences
  each open with 所以 or 因此 has lost its order.
- **The end of the sentence.** Put the new information last; the known comes first and links to what came before.
- **Set phrases.** A four-character phrase or idiom in its dictionary sense, where it says the thing more exactly; not
  two in a row, never as ornament.
- **Positive form.** State what is done and why; a negation carries only a limit that matters.

## Composition (谋篇布局)

- **The chapter.** It opens with a scene that poses its question (the lead line asks it), states its principle once
  with the reason, gives the limits and the practice, and ends on its point, not on a trailing caveat or a summary of
  what was just said.
- **Order.** Sections and paragraphs follow the order a reader needs: what, why, how, where it stops, then hands-on. A
  term is defined before its first use.
- **The paragraph.** One point, carried by its first sentence; the rest supports it. No paragraph restates the one
  before.
- **Transitions.** A paragraph joins the previous one by its content; 下面、接下来、此外 do not stand in for a link.
- **Proportion.** A passage's length matches its weight in the argument; a minor rule is not given a long paragraph.
- **Headings.** Within a chapter, parallel in form and short; each says what its section establishes. Before renaming
  one, search the book for it quoted as 「“…”一节」 and change those references in the same edit.
- **Prose or table.** A table when items are compared on the same dimensions; prose when reasoning joins them.
- **Scope of a move.** Within a chapter, paragraphs and sections may move. A move across chapters or parts is proposed
  to the owner, not made.
- **Length.** The edition stays within its page ceiling; a file is kept the same length or shorter, and a sentence added
  for structure pays for itself with a weaker one cut in the same chapter.
