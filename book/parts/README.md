# Manuscript parts

The book argues that producing a result now outruns understanding it, and
teaches a team how to think, act and collaborate under that condition. One line
runs through the book: the definition of work yields four unavoidable
conditions and three working criteria; responsibility stays with people, so
understanding stays with people; and the same question, how understanding is
reached, is then asked at three scales (one person, after handing over, several
people) before returning to the person. The reading order is declared in
[manuscript.typ](../manuscript.typ).

The parts, their names and the chapter modules each holds are declared once in [manuscript.typ](../manuscript.typ);
each chapter's head and the contents' groups are derived from that declaration. The six parts are: the root; one
person; the Agent; after handing over; several people; the person. The epilogue, the afterword and the appendices
follow them.

Each chapter opens with a situation from real work, explains the principle,
shows it in a case, states what the team does because of it, and names where the
principle stops holding. Cases are rewritten: no internal person, vendor, system
or figure appears. Practice chapters name the companion exercise that backs each
worked example.

The third part explains the Agent through its first six layers: model, harness,
context, tools, boundaries and memory; orchestration and evaluation close the
fourth part. The `first` chapter takes the reader through a first session in a
directory of their own; the third part's other chapters apart from its practice,
the chapters from `run` to `evaluate`, and `shared` end with a hands-on section
run in such a directory, as appendix A lists. A tool is explained by the layer
it sits in and the problem it solves, whether or not the author operated it; an
event is told only as the record has it, and numbers in cases follow the record.
